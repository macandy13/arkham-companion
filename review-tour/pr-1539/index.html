Lookahead bot that tries each step ahead

4 stops2 questions130 of 512 changed lines on the tour

The problem

The goal bot picks among the steps that move the game on at random. It moves to a study it can't investigate, walks off with the clues an act needs at one location, and takes resources it has no use for. On 120 games it reaches a resolution once and draws about 9 encounter cards a game. It also answered half of all enemy attacks with the skip that a Dodge stands for, so many attacks were never played.

What changes

The lookahead bot tries each step the goal bot would ever take on a copy of the table. The goal bot's best picks play out the rest of that turn, and the board at the end is scored: a resolution, acts advanced, clues, how near the next clues are, harm and enemies. The best step is played. The copy comes from a new PlayTable.fork(), which copies state and log without replaying them.

Questions for you

  1. The PR smoke run (100 games, seed 1) now takes about 100 s instead of about 46 s, since a fifth of its games are lookahead games. Keep them in the smoke mix?

    Picked: Keep them: they reach a resolution in 6 of their 23 games and play the later acts the other bots rarely see. The other option leaves the bot to the nightly run only..

  2. The goal and deck bots no longer skip enemy attacks without a Dodge, so their games get harder (goal bot resolutions went from 6 of 200 to 1 of 120). Keep that?

    Picked: Keep it: a skipped attack hides the attack code from the run. The other option keeps the free skips..

Each step is tried ahead and the best one is played

A step the goal bot weighs above 0 is played on a fork, then the goal bot's heaviest picks play on until the turn passes or 12 steps. Tests on the fork draw from their own random, so the bot doesn't see the token its real test draws. A refused step scores lowest. A step whose look ahead crashes the engine scores highest, so the bot walks into the bug and the real step reports it.

How a turn's end is scored

A resolution outweighs everything, an advanced act or agenda the clues and harm of a turn. Clues count where the group holds them, where an act needs them held, and on revealed locations; each move nearer to the next clues counts too. Harm weighs four times as much within 2 of defeat.

4API

A table copy without a replay

States are never changed in place, so a fork shares the current state and copies the log array. PlayTable.resume would replay the whole log for every step tried.
