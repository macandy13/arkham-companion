Card actions read the ability condition

3 stops1 question71 of 296 changed lines on the tour

The problem

Card actions such as Lita Chantler's parley or the Smoke and Mirrors act's action had their own small condition language (ActionCondition), next to the AbilityCondition that every other ability reads. A new card action that needed, say, a count of investigators or a phase had to grow the private language instead of using what triggered abilities already have.

What changes

Card actions now take an AbilityCondition, read in the window of the action itself, which names the acting investigator as you(). The shared condition gains what actions needed: whose turn it is (turn), whether an investigator controls a card (controls), and combinations (allOf, anyOf, not), so every ability can use them.

Question for you

  1. The task suggested turning the earned-card trust of "if you control Lita Chantler" into a reference. It stays inside the controls condition, because a reference names cards the game tracks, and Lita is often in a deck the game never sees. Is that the right home?

Whose turn it is, who controls a card, and the three combinators join AbilityCondition. Every ability can now say "during your turn" or "if there are exactly 1 or 2 investigators and it is your turn".

Controlling a card the game may not track

An investigator controls a card when a copy is in their play area. With no copy in the game at all, the card is in a deck the players hold, so the game trusts them if the campaign log says they earned it, or always in a standalone game.

Actions read the condition in their own window

An action's condition is read in a window that names the acting investigator and the card, so you() and self() mean the same as in a triggered ability.
