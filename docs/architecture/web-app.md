# Web app

The React PWA is a thin layer over the engine.
[`App.tsx`](../../apps/web/src/App.tsx) defines the hash routes; screens are in [`screens/`](../../apps/web/src/screens/):

- home, lying on the table rather than a paper page: the app's title ([`HomeTitle`](../../apps/web/src/screens/HomeTitle.tsx), our own SVG in the spirit of the game's boxes: worn Cinzel Decorative capitals, a brass arch with a star, a COMPANION ribbon, tentacles), a tagline, and the three ways in (New campaign as the brass primary, One scenario, Load a saved game as a pill around the file input), then the shelf ([`shelf.ts`](../../apps/web/src/screens/shelf.ts)): one paper cover per campaign, its scenario games included (`shelfEntries`), and one per game of one scenario, last played first. A campaign cover's button follows where it stands (`campaignStanding`): Continue its chapter's game, Set up chapter N from the log, or Read the log once it is over; its ⋮ menu opens the log meanwhile, renames the campaign, exports it with its games, and deletes it with them after a question (`GameStore.deleteCampaign`). Both covers rename in place with the same form ([`CoverRename`](../../apps/web/src/screens/CoverRename.tsx)). A game's cover has Continue and a ⋮ menu ([`CoverMenu`](../../apps/web/src/screens/CoverMenu.tsx)) to rename, duplicate, export or delete it. Each cover opens with a band of art ([`CoverArt`](../../apps/web/src/screens/CoverArt.tsx)): the art of its scenario's first agenda, cut as the play screen's agenda cuts it and loaded from ArkhamDB at runtime (a campaign shows its chapter at hand, or its last once past them), plain paper until it loads or when offline. A campaign's cover lays its box's key art over that, the `og:image` of the campaign's FFG product page ([`campaignKeyArt`](../../apps/web/src/catalog/campaignKeyArt.ts)), loaded from FFG's image host at runtime and never bundled; the agenda art stands in while it loads or when it can't. Each cover shows its team as round portraits ([`TeamPortraits`](../../apps/web/src/screens/TeamPortraits.tsx)): the art cut from each investigator's card picture, which home finds in the card catalog once it has loaded, their initial until then. The cover played last wears a "Last played" ribbon and its button is the brass primary,
- new game wizard, whose campaign path starts a campaign log and its first scenario as one of its games (`AppState.startCampaign`),
- campaign log (`/campaign/:id`, [`campaign/`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/)),
- game screen,
- settings (MD.3c), written like a form of the campaign guide: one row per subject (Collection, Languages, Table, Cards) with its label in spaced capitals and its fields as lines to write on, the table's day or night as a segmented pill, then the unofficial fan project notice; on a phone each label stands over its fields. The collection is one line there, the owned boxes and how many campaigns, that opens its own page (`/settings/collection`, [`CollectionScreen`](../../apps/web/src/screens/CollectionScreen.tsx)) with a tick per pack of each campaign the app plays (`CampaignEntry.packCodes`). Both pages save on every change through [`useSettings`](../../apps/web/src/settings/useSettings.ts).

Over every screen outside play, the [app bar](../../apps/web/src/screens/AppBar.tsx) (`AppBarFrame`, MD.3a) lies on the table: a back chevron up to the screen above, the screen's name in the display face over a subtitle, and a ⋮ menu with the app's places (Your games, Settings). The bar sits outside the screen's paper page (`#app > main`), so each screen names itself with `useAppBar({ title, subtitle, up })` while it is mounted; home names none, so its bar holds only the menu under the home screen's own title. The chevron goes to `up`, a route, not back in history, so a deep link still has a way up. The play screen has no bar: its status row's menu holds the same places.

Components read the [app store](../../apps/web/src/state/appStore.ts) and call its methods; they never change game state or touch storage.

The wizard's first step also asks how much the players rely on the helpers (`Reliance`, M1C.2d: "What the app tells you"): every change it makes, the changes to investigators and enemies and the cards drawn, or only what they do by hand. A quick game keeps the level as its own, a campaign for all its games (`ScenarioStart.reliance`). `OpenGame.reliance` is the campaign's for a game of a campaign, else the game's; the game log changes it (`AppState.setReliance`), a setting outside the steps, so undo never takes it back. The changes notice shows only the lines of kinds the level shows (`showsNotice`). The drawn cards and what to do by hand show at every level: the drawn-cards notice is where the players place a card whose revelation the engine left to them.

## Investigator picker

The new game wizard's [investigator picker](../../apps/web/src/picker/InvestigatorPicker.tsx) shows the catalog's investigators alphabetically in pages of ten ([`PortraitPages.tsx`](../../apps/web/src/picker/PortraitPages.tsx)):

- one list, laid out by the stylesheet in two rows of five per page (the first five of every ten in the top row), which scrolls sideways and snaps a page at a time;
- each portrait is the figure cut from the picture of the card's front (`--picker-portrait-*` in tokens.css) over the name, the class in its colour (`--color-class-*`) and the skills, health and sanity as icons with values.

Its filters sit behind one round button that opens a menu, drawn like the play screen's ☰ menu (`button[data-icon][aria-expanded] + *` in base.css), and the button counts the filters set.
A tap on a portrait opens the detail sheet as a dialog.

## Campaign log

The [campaign screen](../../apps/web/src/campaign/CampaignScreen.tsx) draws the open campaign (`AppState.openCampaign`, `OpenCampaign`: the replayed `CampaignLog` and the campaign's games) like the Core Set's campaign log sheet (docs/product.md "Campaign screen"):

- an index card with the printed fields in a column per investigator and blank columns up to the printed four, all on shared subgrid rows so labels sit level (unspent XP, trauma as tally marks ([`Tally.tsx`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/Tally.tsx)), earned story cards by name from the catalog), and a napkin with the campaign's scenarios, the played ones ticked with their resolution and the next one marked `aria-current`;
- notepad pages ([`CampaignSheets.tsx`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/CampaignSheets.tsx)) with the campaign notes and the campaign's own sheets from its `CampaignLogLayout`, each record in words from `CampaignEntry.storyKey` (a flag's note at `<storyKey>.<record>.yes` or `.no`, a list's heading at `<storyKey>.<record>`), crossed-out names in `<del>`;
- the sheets every campaign shares after them: a newspaper clipping with the killed and insane investigators (replaced ones first) and an index card with the chaos bag, each kind of token once with its count, in `CHAOS_TOKENS` order;
- a link that continues the game of the scenario in play.

The paper look is tokens, so dialogs can share it: fonts `--font-family-print`, `-log-label`, `-script`, `-hand`; inks `--color-print`, `-pen`, `-script-ink`, `-stamp`, `--color-newsprint-ink`; papers `--index-card-paper`, `--napkin-paper`, `--clipping-paper`, `--notepad-paper` (ruled `--notepad-line` apart), tilts `--sheet-tilt`, `--clipping-tilt`; the torn outline `--paper-edge` (a `clip-path` polygon on a layer behind the sheet, so the writing stays crisp).
[`campaign_log.css`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/campaign_log.css) selects by the sheets' ids, and `base.css` leaves it off the paper the other screens are printed on.

When a game ends, `OpenGame.resolution` (`PendingResolution`) holds the resolution it reached and, for each of its scenario's resolutions and "no resolution", what `campaign.proposeResolution` proposes (`ResolutionOption`), read against its campaign's stored log: a log whose current scenario is another one has written the game already, so nothing is pending. `AppState.writeResolution` takes the commands the players confirmed and appends `campaign.applyResolution`'s events as one campaign undo step, all or none. It remembers that step as `AppState.writtenResolution` (campaign and last `seq`), and the campaign screen offers to take it back (`ResolutionUndo`): `undoResolution` calls `GameStore.undoLastCampaign` with that `seq`, so a log changed since refuses instead of losing a later change, and the ended game proposes its resolution again. A standalone game proposes its resolution too, with no campaign to write it into.

Over an ended game, [`ResolutionPrompt`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/ResolutionPrompt.tsx) opens [`ResolutionDialog`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/ResolutionDialog.tsx) in two steps. "The scenario is over" lists the resolutions with the one the game reached tagged "from the game", for the players to confirm or change; "Into the campaign log" names the resolution to read aloud from the guide, lists the changes grouped like the log page (campaign notes, investigators, chaos bag, then the by-hand steps "At the table"), lets the players pick an option of each choice, and after the write goes to the campaign screen. It waits while the board is being adjusted, and "Not yet" closes it until the game is opened again. A standalone game gets the same steps, titled "Resolution steps", and "Done" closes them without writing.

An interlude (MC1.6e) is written the same way from the campaign screen. `OpenCampaign.interlude` is the one `campaign.findDueInterlude` finds after the last played scenario, looked up in the campaign's definition (`createAppStore`'s `CampaignLookup`, `findCampaign` in the app, so tests can add one). While it is due and the next scenario has no game yet, the screen offers it instead of the setup. [`InterludeDialog`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/InterludeDialog.tsx) names it (`<storyKey>.interludes.<id>`) to read aloud, asks each decision one at a time (`campaign.proposeInterlude`, "Back" takes an answer back), then lists the changes like the resolution. Both dialogs draw the list with [`ProposedChanges`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/ProposedChanges.tsx). `AppState.writeInterlude` appends `campaign.applyInterlude`'s events as one undo step, and `writtenResolution.kind` lets the same toast say which was written and take it back. No campaign the app plays has an interlude yet, so the E2E build (`vite build --mode e2e`) adds a test campaign with one ([`interlude_test_campaign.ts`](../../apps/web/src/scenarios/interlude_test_campaign.ts)), which `findCampaign` finds but the wizard never offers; the app's own build leaves it out.

Once the log has moved on to a scenario that has no game yet, the log's footer sets it up instead of continuing: [`NextScenarioDialog`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/NextScenarioDialog.tsx) previews the difficulty and team the log sets it up with, each investigator's unspent experience to spend on deck upgrades at the table, and what the log changes in the setup ("Your house burned to the ground." → "The investigators start at Rivertown", from the engine's `storyReadsOfSetup` worded by [`storyReads.ts`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/storyReads.ts); a flag with no note of its own is left out), then `AppState.startNextScenario` (`NextScenarioStart`) sets the scenario up from the stored log (`CampaignSetupOptions`, the team in the log's order) as a new game of the campaign and opens it. Like the wizard, the dialog takes the team's card stats from the catalog. A killed or insane team member needs a replacement before Start: the dialog opens [`ReplacementPicker`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/ReplacementPicker.tsx), the investigator picker over only the investigators the campaign hasn't had ([`replacements.ts`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/replacements.ts)), and `NextScenarioStart.replacements` writes the `ReplaceInvestigator` commands into the log before setup. With nobody left to choose, "End the campaign" sends `EndCampaign { result: 'lost' }` (`AppState.loseCampaign`), and the index card says how the campaign ended (`CampaignOver`, MC1.6f): a red stamp with the result (won, lost or survived) and the day of `CampaignEnded` (`CampaignSummary.endedAt`, the stored event's timestamp), then which ending of the last scenario to read in the campaign guide when that scenario's resolution ended it. The log stays readable from the home screen, whose campaign row then links "Read the log" instead of "Resume". The campaign dialogs share [`campaign_dialogs.css`](../../apps/web/src/campaign/campaign_dialogs.css).

## Open game

The open game (`OpenGame`) is the play screen's single source of truth:

- **`GameActions`** ([`gameActions.ts`](../../apps/web/src/state/gameActions.ts)) are the players' verbs (`moveTo`, `investigate`, `answer('pass')`). They act for the investigator whose turn it is and answer the first pending decision, so they take no ids.
  - Investigate, engage, take a resource, draw and play (`takeResource`, `drawCard`, `playCard`: the engine's commands with `asAction` set), and move, fight and evade, also count off what the action costs that investigator (`PlayTable.spendActionFor`, `BoardView.actionCost`: one, more while a card raises it, as Frozen in Fear does), naming it so the engine knows the round's first; the engine leaves counting to the players. A `free` move, fight or evade counts off only what a card adds to it and provokes no attacks of opportunity; the pawn's drag (`moveTo` without a cost) counts nothing, as it also catches up with a move made at the table. A test stays open for the decision dialog;
  - buttons are disabled while `OpenGame.busy`.
- **`GameSession`** ([`gameSession.ts`](../../apps/web/src/state/gameSession.ts)) holds a `PlayTable` resumed from the stored log, plays each action as one undo group, writes it, then publishes the state.
  - A refused step stores nothing and shows its `Rejection` as a toast; a failed write reloads the stored game.
  - Steps run strictly in order.
  - `undoTo(step)` goes back to right after a step of the game log by taking back each later undo group in turn, so storage and memory step back exactly as with Undo.
  - While the players adjust the board (`startAdjusting`, `OpenGame.adjusting`), steps change the table in memory only; "Done" (`finishAdjusting`) plays them again from where the adjustment started as one stored step, so one undo takes the whole correction back, and "Cancel" resumes the table from that point.
  - The screen shows the steppers and the location details' corrections only while adjusting (`data-adjusting` on the board) and holds back the steps of play (turn controls, the act's advance, piece drags) meanwhile.
- **`BoardView`** (`OpenGame.board`) is what the screen reads: names, thresholds and derived values, never raw state.
  The encounter piles come from `BoardView.encounter` (the deck's count, the discard pile top first, the set-aside cards and the victory display, each card with its id, code, name, type, picture and victory points), so the screen never reads zones.

The play screen is described in [Play screen](play-screen.md) and its pages.

## Strings

UI strings live in [`i18n/`](../../apps/web/src/i18n/), one folder per language: `app.ts` holds the app's own strings, and each campaign has a file of its own (`night_of_the_zealot.ts`) with its name, scenario names, scenario choices and story records. [`index.ts`](../../apps/web/src/i18n/index.ts) joins them by section (`CAMPAIGN_SECTIONS`: `campaigns`, `scenarios`, `scenario`, `story`), so the app's file doesn't grow with every campaign. The tests check that German has every English key and that no two campaigns write the same key.

## Extension points

- **A new game action:** add it to `GameActions` as one `PlayTable` verb (add the verb to `PlayTable` first). Only actions outside the open game get their own `AppState` method.
- **A new screen:** a route in `App.tsx`, a component in `screens/` that names itself in the app bar (`useAppBar`), an E2E flow tagged `@flow` or a screenshot tagged `@shot`, strings in English and German.
- **A new refusal or skippable effect:** its `rejections.<code>` or `skipped.effects.<type>` string in English and German.
- **A new decision prompt or option label:** its string under the engine's key (`decision.<key>`, or the scenario's `scenario.<scenario>.<card>.<key>`) in English and German.
- **A new campaign's strings:** a `<campaign>.ts` file in both `i18n/en/` and `i18n/de/`, holding only the `CAMPAIGN_SECTIONS`, and listed in `campaignTexts`.
