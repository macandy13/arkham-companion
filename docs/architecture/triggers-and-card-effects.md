# Triggers and card effects

Forced effects are **abilities**: data on a card's `CardStats`, so card data holds no code and the same card behaves the same in every scenario. A scenario's own rules are abilities of its agendas and acts. An [`Ability`](../../packages/engine/src/abilities.ts) is a timing window (`revealed`, `entered`, `turnBegins`, `turnEnds`, `eachTurnEnds` (any investigator's turn, for a card in play), `phaseEnds`, `roundEnds`, `advanced`, `enemyDefeated`, `tested` after a basic action's test, `engaged` either way round, `attacked` (attacks of opportunity included), `cluesDiscovered`, `defeated` for the enemy itself, naming where it was, `tokensPlaced` for tokens placed on the card; setup opens none), an optional condition (the current act, the tokens on the card itself: `whenTokensOnSelf('clue', perInvestigator(2), advanceAct())` for an objective on the act's own clues, whose count the act's view shows as its threshold, what the window's investigator has: `ifYouHave('clues', { max: 0 }, …)`, or how many enemies of a trait are in play: `ifEnemiesInPlay(trait('Cultist'), { max: 0 }, …)`), an optional repeat once per matching enemy (`forEachEnemy({ trait, at }, …)`, counted with the condition as the window opens), and a list of effects. "Take 1 horror and 1 damage" is one `TakeDamageAndHorror` (`takeDamageAndHorror({ damage, horror })`), placed at once through the same `woundEvents` as an enemy attack, so a defeat by one does not lose the other. An effect that leaves an enemy unengaged next to an investigator ends with `engageReadyEnemies()` (`EngageReadyEnemies`), which runs the engagement rule for every enemy at a location, as setup does. "This card gains surge" (`GainSurge`) marks the resolving card (`CardInstance.gainedSurge`), and placing it queues the surge draw a printed surge would have queued. `windowsOpenedBy` maps an event to the windows it opens.

Effects are **templated commands**: a command card data may use (`AbilityCommand`) with every ID replaced by a reference relative to the ability (`self`, `windowInvestigator`, `lead`, `onlyCopy(code)`, `eachInvestigator`, `eachEnemy({ trait, withoutTrait, unengaged, engagedWith })`, `nearestEnemy(trait)`, `locationWith(trait)`, `eachSetAside(trait)`, …); an `each…` reference repeats the command once per investigator or card. When its turn comes, `resolveEffect` fills in the IDs from the current state, so handlers and the log only see plain commands. A reference with candidates (`nearestEnemy`, `locationWith`) stands for the only one, or becomes a decision with one option per candidate and the effect filled in for it: equally near enemies are the lead investigator's `chooseCard`, a location to move to is the moving investigator's `chooseLocation`. A test's outcomes stay templated until the answer: each runs as a `ResolveEffect` command, which `handle` turns into an `ability` step, so it resolves in its own turn like any effect (a choice's options, by contrast, resolve when it is raised, so they can be dry-run). A reference that names nobody drops the command; a missing `onlyCopy` card records `AbilityEffectSkipped` and the players do it by hand. Commands on player cards (`DrawCards`, `SearchDeck`, `PlayCard`; factories `drawCards`, `searchDeck`; and `DiscardFromHand`, which only a `handCards` cost emits) are written as if the engine could carry them out; their handler decides it can't, since the game holds no player cards, and records `CommandDeferred`, which the play screen lists with the skipped effects for the players to do, unless the players took it as an action themselves (`asAction`). The basic draw and play actions are these commands taken `asAction`, and the resource action is `GainResources` (which card effects use too) taken `asAction`, so all three provoke attacks of opportunity like the actions the engine carries out. Supporting player cards later only changes those handlers. Factories write effects the way the card reads:

```ts
whenRevealed(takeHorror(1))
atEndOfTurn(skillTest('willpower', 3, { onPass: [discardSelf()] }))
afterTest('investigate', 'pass', discardSelf())   // Obscuring Fog
whenAdvanced(revealLocation(onlyCopy(codes.parlor)), …)
```

A card's test can cost the tester for each point it fails by (`skillTest(…, { perPointFailed })`: 1 damage, 1 horror, or 1 of their clues placed on their location by `PlaceClues`); the fail answer's `margin` is the count. "Test [willpower] (3) or [agility] (3)" is `skillTestOfEither(id, ['willpower', 'agility'], 3, outcomes)`: a choice for the tester with one `skillTest` per skill, the same outcomes either way. `PlaceTokens` puts clues from the token pool on a card in play (`placeCluesOnSelf(1)`, "place 1 clue on this act"); doom keeps its own `PlaceDoom`.

Action abilities ("[action]: …") are data too: `CardStats.actions` lists [`CardAction`](../../packages/engine/src/card_actions.ts)s, each a list of effects and an optional designator (`resign`, `parley`). `UseCardAction` takes one in the investigator's own turn where the card offers it (`offersActionsTo`: a location's at it, an enemy's at its location, a threat area's at its owner's location, the current agenda's and act's for anyone) and continues with its effects as ability steps in an `action` window naming the investigator. An action ability provokes attacks of opportunity unless it is a Resign or Parley, which is why `CommandSpec.provokes` and `continueWith` see the context and state. A `CardAction.limit` ("once per turn", "once per game", group limit) is checked against the uses `CardActionUsed` records on the card (`CardInstance.actionUses`, with who took it and in which round; `withinLimit`), so `cardActionsOfferedTo` leaves out an action used up. A `CardAction.cost` ("Spend 5 resources:", `ActionCost`) is paid by `UseCardAction` before the effects; an action that can't be paid for (`canPay`) is neither offered nor accepted (`cardActionCostUnpaid`). A group cost (`groupClues`, "2 clues per investigator, as a group") is paid by any active investigators: `UseCardAction.spend` names who pays how many (`groupCluePayments`), and without it the taker pays what they can and the others the rest (`defaultCluePayments`). A cost the game can't pay itself (`handCards`, "choose and discard 4 cards from your hand") is always payable to the engine and paid by a deferred `DiscardFromHand`. A free action (`CardAction.free`, a "[free]" ability) costs no action: `UseCardAction` takes it in any player window, outside the taker's turn too, and it never provokes (`UseCardAction` has no `asAction`: the printed ability decides); `GameActions.useCardAction` counts off an action only for the others. A `CardAction.condition` ("If you control Lita Chantler:", `ActionCondition`) must hold for the taker (`actionConditionHolds`), else the action is neither offered nor accepted (`cardActionConditionUnmet`); `controls` checks their play area, and when the game has no copy of the card at all it is a player card the game doesn't track, so the taker is trusted, as with a hand card cost, provided the campaign log says someone earned it (`earnedWhen`, a `StoryCondition`: The Gathering records `litaEarned`, so Umôrdhoth's action is hidden when nobody earned Lita and in a standalone game). `investigatorTurn` ("during any investigator's turn") holds while anyone's turn is in progress: `advanceBySpendingClues(clues)` is the act's group clue spend as a free action with that condition, a `groupClues` cost and `AdvanceAct`, which the act builders give every act with a clue threshold (after its printed actions, so their indices stay). `AdvanceAct` itself pays nothing: it is that action's effect, an objective's or another card's. `BoardView.act.advance` says how clues advance the act (`ActAdvance`): `spendClues`, the clues the investigators hold against that cost (`cluesToAdvance`), or `cluesOnAct`, an objective counting the clues on it (`tokenObjective`); `PlayTable.spendCluesToAdvanceAct` takes the action for the investigator whose turn it is. A rejected effect rejects the whole action, cost included. `resignAction()` is the Resign of the Parlor, the Main Path and The Midnight Masks' agendas; an investigator resigns only through such a card, so `PlayTable` and `GameActions` offer no plain resign. `parleyAction({ cost, limit, effects })` marks any card action a Parley; its consequences are ordinary effects, so a cultist's "Parley. Add it to the victory display" is `addToVictoryDisplay({ ref: 'self' })` and another card's Parley can test, discard or take a cost with the same blocks. A story asset an investigator takes control of (Lita Chantler's Parley at the Parlor, `takeControl()`) moves to their `playArea` zone: it counts as in play, `InvestigatorView.controls` lists it, it offers no actions (its abilities are the controller's to use by hand, like a player card's), and it is removed from the game when they leave the scenario (`investigatorLeavesEvents`). `BoardView.cardActions(investigator)` lists the actions the cards in play offer them (`cardActionsOfferedTo`), which the turn row and the card screens show as buttons; when a card prints more than one action of a kind (two plain ones, two Parleys) each gets a `number` over the printed actions, so its button has a name of its own ("Action 2 · Parlor") that stays put when another drops out. The act's spend-clues advance is left out of that list and of the numbering, since `act.advance` shows it.

Constant abilities are data too (`CardStats.modifiers`); [`effectiveValue`](../../packages/engine/src/card_values.ts) is the one place that answers "what is this value now".

## Extension points

- **A card's ability, action or modifier:** list it in the card's data; no code.
- **A new window or condition:** a case in `TimingWindow` / `windowsOpenedBy` or `AbilityCondition` / `actionConditionHolds`.
- **A new kind of effect:** add its command to `AbilityCommand` (and a factory); a new reference is a case in `resolveRefs`.
